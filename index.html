How it works

No riddles. No trick logic. Just you and a pencil.

Every clue is a simple yes-or-no test you can check against a single row. Work them in order or read them all first. You'll land in the same place either way.

Search the ledger

Thousands of records: sales, tickets or names, each with its own details and number.

Eliminate thousands

Sixteen to twenty-two clues, each with a piece of the story. Every one crosses out a fresh chunk.

Narrow it down

Eight to ten suspects survive. Ordinary-looking, every one of them.

Find the killer

The final clue compares the survivors. Unlock the confession and find out if you were right.

Try a mini case

One candle shop. Sixteen sales. Four postcards.

A pocket-sized case in the style of the books. Tap a row to cross it out, and tap again to bring it back. When one row is left, you'll find out if you caught the killer's purchase.

  1. 1No Set, Kit, Pack, Pair, Trio or Sampler.The killer wanted one thing, wrapped and done. No bundles.
  2. 2The price is $40.00 or less.The card on file had $40 left on it that night.
  3. 3The name has letters and spaces only.The torn receipt shows the item line. No numbers, no &, no punctuation.
  4. 4The transaction number's last digit differs from the price's last digit.The register voided sales with matching endings. The killer's went through.

A price's last digit is its last cents digit, so $24.00 ends in 0.

About the author

Say it fast: Eli M. Nate.

Eli M. Nate writes murder mysteries you solve yourself, one crossed-out row at a time. The pen name is the whole method.

Every case starts with an ordinary crowd: shoppers at a mall, fans at a concert, guests at a party. Somewhere in their records is the one the killer left behind. The clues are fair and checkable, and they never ask you to guess what the author was thinking.

Every book is tested so no clue lets you skip a chapter and every reader who does the work lands on the same answer.
